## Life

Lucius Annaeus Seneca was born at Corduba in Spain, made a career at
Rome, was exiled to Corsica under Claudius, was recalled to tutor the
young Nero, and became with Burrus the effective manager of the early
years of Nero's reign. He withdrew as his position became untenable and
was ordered to take his own life in 65 CE after the Pisonian conspiracy.

## The problem his career poses

Seneca wrote on the contempt for wealth while being among the richest men
in Rome, on clemency while serving an increasingly murderous emperor, and
on the good death while accommodating himself for years to a court that
made accommodation costly.

Ancient critics noticed. So did he: several of the letters address the
gap between what he recommends and what he manages, and the honesty of
that admission is part of why the letters are still read.

This platform does not resolve the tension. It is the most interesting
thing about him.

## The works

The *Letters to Lucilius* are the most read: short essays in epistolary
form on time, death, friendship, anger and the practice of philosophy as
daily discipline rather than doctrine. The dialogues include *On Anger*,
*On the Shortness of Life* and *On Providence*. The tragedies — *Medea*,
*Thyestes*, *Phaedra* — are extraordinarily violent and were decisive for
Renaissance drama.

## Why he appears in the architecture layer

For a single letter. In *Letters* 56 he describes, from the flat above a
bath-house, the noise of grunting weightlifters, the slap of the masseur,
the shouting of the drink seller and the man who sings in the plunge
pool. It is the best surviving account of what a Roman bath actually
sounded like, and it comes from a philosopher complaining about the
racket while trying to work.
