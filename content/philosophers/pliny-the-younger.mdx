## The letters

Gaius Plinius Caecilius Secundus published nine books of letters,
carefully selected and polished for publication, covering law, politics,
literature, estate management, patronage and friendship. A tenth book
preserves his official correspondence with Trajan as governor of
Bithynia-Pontus, with the emperor's replies — a unique record of Roman
provincial administration in operation.

## Vesuvius

Two letters to Tacitus, written some twenty-five years after the event,
describe the eruption of 79 CE. The first recounts the death of his
uncle, Pliny the Elder, who took ships across the bay both to observe the
phenomenon and to evacuate people, and died on the shore. The second
describes the writer's own escape from Misenum with his mother.

They are the only eyewitness account of the eruption, and they are the
reason the eruption column type is named Plinian. They are also
retrospective literary compositions written at Tacitus's request for a
history, which is worth remembering when they are quoted as reportage.

The date they give, in the manuscripts as transmitted, is 24 August. A
substantial body of physical evidence points to autumn, and many
specialists now treat the transmitted date as a manuscript corruption.

## Why he appears in the architecture layer

For his villa letters. Two long descriptions of his own houses, room by
room, with orientation, views, heating and the reasons for each
arrangement, are the fullest account of how a Roman of means thought
about domestic space — and a useful corrective to reading villa plans
without knowing what they were for.
