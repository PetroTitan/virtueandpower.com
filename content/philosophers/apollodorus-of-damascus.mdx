## The works attributed

Apollodorus is credited by ancient sources with the Forum of Trajan and
its basilica, the column, the bridge over the Danube built for the Dacian
campaign, and works including baths at Rome. A surviving treatise on
siege engines is transmitted under his name.

The Danube bridge is the most remarkable of these and does not survive.
It is depicted on Trajan's Column and described by Cassius Dio, who
regarded it as the most impressive of Trajan's works; the piers are
reported to have been visible in the river long afterwards.

## The quarrel with Hadrian

Cassius Dio tells a story in which Apollodorus dismissed a design of
Hadrian's, was resented for it, and was eventually banished and put to
death after criticising the emperor's temple of Venus and Roma.

The story is entertaining and is not well supported. Dio wrote a century
later, the anecdote fits a familiar pattern of the artist punished for
candour, and nothing corroborates it. This platform records it as a
tradition rather than as a fact about how Apollodorus died.

## Why the attributions matter

Roman architectural authorship is better attested than Greek, but not by
much, and it is still mostly literary. Apollodorus is the most prominent
named Roman architect after Vitruvius, and even in his case the
connection to the buildings rests on later writers rather than on
inscriptions from the works themselves.
