## The question is usually asked badly

"Is the Odyssey historical?" cannot be answered as put. Answer yes and
you have converted a poem into a chronicle; answer no and you have
thrown away a boar's-tusk helmet, a set of genuinely old names, and a
site identification that is probably correct.

The poem is a traditional composition that carries material of several
periods at once. The only workable method is to stop asking about the
poem and start asking about individual claims, one at a time, with the
evidence for each stated.

That is what this guide does, and what the rest of the cluster does.
Every claim carries one of five labels.

## The layers

The single most important fact about the Odyssey as a historical
document is that it is not one document. In the same passage you can
find:

- objects that had gone out of use centuries before the poem;
- social practices of the intervening centuries;
- institutions contemporary with the poet;
- formulaic language older than any of the above;
- and outright invention.

They cannot be peeled apart cleanly because the tradition never kept
them separate. A reader who selects one layer and calls it the poem's
"real" period has constructed a historical Homer that never existed.

## Why the layering happens

An oral tradition transmits phrases and typical scenes over long
periods. A phrase that fits the metre survives even when the object it
names has gone out of use, because the phrase is a tool. So a poet
composing in the eighth century can describe a helmet nobody has worn
for four hundred years, in a formula inherited from singers who did
see one, while giving his characters iron tools because that is what
tools are made of now.

That is not confusion on the poem's part. It is how the medium works.
See <a href="/homer/oral-tradition">Oral tradition and the making of the poems</a>.

## What follows for criticism

Two rules govern the rest of this platform's Homer material.

**Nothing here converts the poem into a history.** The characters live
under <a href="/figures">Figures of the tradition</a>, each stating its
historicity explicitly, rather than under the historical figures.

**Charges of "historical inaccuracy" against adaptations must specify
what they are inaccurate to.** A production that departs from Homer has
departed from a poem. A production that departs from the Late Bronze
Age has departed from an archaeological reconstruction that Homer
himself departs from constantly. These are different criticisms with
different force. Running them together is the single most common error
in popular writing on the subject, and it is one this platform does not
make. See
<a href="/films/christopher-nolan-the-odyssey/historical-accuracy">the film's historical accuracy</a>.
