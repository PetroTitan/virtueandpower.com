## The problem the poem sets

A man leaves for twenty years. His son was an infant and does not know
his face. His wife has been told he is dead by a series of liars. His
appearance has been altered by hardship and then deliberately disguised
by a goddess. What could establish that he is who he says he is?

The second half of the *Odyssey* is a systematic answer to that
question, and the answers are all different.

## The recognitions, and their mechanisms

**Telemachus (Book 16) — by declaration.** No proof at all. Odysseus is
transformed by Athena, his son takes him for a god, and he simply says
that he is his father. Telemachus initially refuses to believe it. It
is the weakest evidence in the poem and it works, because the two have
no shared past to draw on.

**Argos the dog (Book 17) — by scent and instinct.** Immediate, certain,
and useless: the dog cannot speak, and dies.

**Eurycleia (Book 19) — by the body.** She finds the boar-scar on his
thigh while washing his feet. The poem then breaks off for some seventy
lines to tell how he got the scar, hunting on Parnassus, and how his
grandfather named him. It is involuntary recognition, and it is
immediately suppressed by force: he takes her by the throat.

**Eumaeus and Philoetius (Book 21) — by the scar, shown deliberately.**
The same evidence, offered rather than discovered, as credentials
before the killing.

**Penelope (Book 23) — by a shared secret.** She orders the marriage
bed moved; he explains, angrily, that he built it around a living olive
trunk rooted in the ground. The proof is a fact only two living people
knew.

**Laertes (Book 24) — by the scar and by memory.** The scar again, and
then the trees: thirteen pear, ten apple, forty fig, named by a man
recalling what his father gave him when he was a boy in the garden.

## What the sequence argues

The mechanisms are ranked, and the ranking is an argument. Physical
marks are the weakest kind of proof, because a body can be counterfeited
in principle and because the scar identifies a body rather than a
person. The bed and the trees are the strongest, because they are
shared history: things that cannot be reported by a third party because
nobody else was there.

The poem's implicit position is that identity is constituted by
relationships and by what two people know together, not by appearance.
This is why Penelope's test is better than Eurycleia's discovery, and
why the poem gives it the climactic position.

## The Greek terms

*Anagnōrisis*, recognition — the word Aristotle takes up in the
*Poetics*, where he classifies its kinds and rates recognition by
tokens as the least artistic and recognition arising from the action
itself as the best. The bed is his kind.

*Sēma*, sign or token — the scar, the bed, the trees. The word also
means a grave-marker, which gives the recognitions a quiet undertow.

## The ethical tension

Recognition in this poem is always preceded by testing, and the testing
is often cruel. Odysseus watches his wife weep, his nurse grieve, and
his father pour dust on his head, in each case while withholding what
would stop it. The poem does not apologise for this and does not
present it as a flaw to be corrected. It is what a man who has survived
by concealment has become.

## Later interpretation

Erich Auerbach's *Mimesis* (1946) opens with the scar passage and uses
it to characterise Homeric style: everything brought into the
foreground and fully illuminated, with no depths and no perspective.
The reading has been contested but it remains the standard entry point
for discussion of how the poem narrates.
