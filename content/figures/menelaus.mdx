## In the Odyssey

Telemachus arrives at Sparta during a double wedding feast and is
received without being asked who he is — correct procedure, and pointed,
since the poem has just shown a household where the procedure has
collapsed.

Menelaus is rich beyond anything Telemachus has seen, and unhappy. He
says he takes no pleasure in his possessions, that he would give up
two-thirds of them to have the men who died at Troy back, and that of
all of them he grieves most for Odysseus.

He tells the story of Proteus: becalmed in Egypt, he was told by the
sea-god's daughter to ambush her father among the seals and hold him
through every transformation until he answered. From Proteus he learned
that Ajax had drowned, that Agamemnon had been murdered at home, and
that Odysseus was alive on an island, held by a nymph.

## The marriage

The most interesting thing in Book 4 is not the information. It is that
Menelaus and Helen tell two incompatible stories about the same events,
in the same room, minutes apart, in front of a guest.

Hers: Odysseus entered Troy disguised as a beggar, she alone recognised
him, she washed him and swore not to betray him, and by then her heart
had already turned back towards home.

His: Odysseus was inside the wooden horse, and Helen walked around it
calling out to the hidden men in the voices of their wives, and would
have destroyed them if Odysseus had not held them silent.

Neither contradicts the other outright and they cannot both be the
whole truth. The poem sets them beside each other and moves on. It is
one of the earliest depictions in European literature of a marriage
conducted through competing accounts of the past.

## Other ancient traditions

The Epic Cycle carried the story of the recovery of Helen at the sack,
in versions where Menelaus intends to kill her and drops the sword.
Herodotus reports an Egyptian tradition in which Helen spent the war in
Egypt and Troy was besieged over a woman who was not there — an
alternative tradition, recorded here as such.

## Later reception

Attic tragedy generally treated him unsympathetically. Euripides'
*Helen* dramatises the Egyptian version.

## In modern adaptation

The 2026 film retains the Sparta visit and gives Menelaus the account of
the inside of the wooden horse. It complicates the reunion with Helen,
which is a legitimate extrapolation from the tension Book 4 leaves
