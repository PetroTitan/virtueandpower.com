## In the Odyssey

She appears once, in Book 11, among the dead. Odysseus does not know
she has died; he left her alive when he sailed for Troy.

She comes to the trench of blood and he does not let her drink until
Tiresias has spoken — a detail readers find hard, and which the poem
records without comment. When she does speak she gives him the state of
his house: Telemachus is managing, Penelope waits and weeps, Laertes
has withdrawn to the country and sleeps in the ashes. And she says how
she died: not by illness, and not by the arrows of Artemis, but of
longing for him.

He tries three times to take her in his arms, and three times she goes
through them like a shadow or a dream. He asks whether she is a phantom
sent by Persephone. She explains the nature of the dead: the sinews no
longer hold flesh and bone, the fire consumes them, and the spirit
flies away like a dream.

## Why the scene matters

This is the poem's definition of death, given by a mother to her son in
the course of a conversation about domestic arrangements. Homeric
afterlife is not punishment or reward for most people; it is
diminishment — the shade retains identity and loses substance.

The three failed embraces are among the oldest images in European
literature of the specific frustration of grief: presence without
contact.

## Other ancient traditions

A later tradition, quite separate from Homer and hostile to Odysseus,
made Sisyphus his true father by seduction of Anticleia before her
marriage to Laertes — a genealogy designed to attribute his cunning to
the archetypal trickster. It appears in the tragedians and in later
mythography. Homer knows nothing of it, and this platform does not
blend the two.

## Later reception

The failed embrace became a set piece. Virgil reuses it twice in the
*Aeneid* — Aeneas with Creusa and with Anchises — in lines that are
close to Homer's, and Dante takes it up again.

## In modern adaptation

The underworld sequence is usually retained for spectacle and usually
loses the mother, because the scene has no plot function: she tells him
nothing he acts on. Its work is entirely emotional.
